## References

**1. Bhattacharya, P., Ghosh, K., Pal, A., & Ghosh, S. (2020). Methods for Computing Legal Document Similarity: A Comparative Study.**
This paper presents the first systematic, common-platform comparison of legal document similarity methods, evaluating both citation-network-based approaches (e.g., bibliographic coupling, co-citation, Node2Vec graph embeddings) and text-based approaches (e.g., Doc2Vec, thematic segment similarity) on Indian Supreme Court case documents. The study finds that combining network and textual similarity outperforms either approach alone, using Pearson correlation against expert-annotated document pairs as the evaluation metric.
[arXiv:2004.12307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12307)

**2. Mandal, A., Ghosh, K., Ghosh, S., & Mandal, S. (2021). Unsupervised Approaches for Measuring Textual Similarity Between Legal Court Case Reports.** *Artificial Intelligence and Law, 29*(3), 417–451.
This paper benchmarks 56 unsupervised text-similarity methods — spanning bag-of-words approaches (TF-IDF, LDA), word and document embeddings (Word2Vec, Doc2Vec), transformer-based models (BERT), and legal-domain embeddings (Law2Vec) — on a large corpus of Indian Supreme Court case documents. The study finds that traditional bag-of-words methods perform competitively with, and in several cases outperform, more computationally expensive context-aware models for this task.
[DOI: 10.1007/s10506-020-09280-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10506-020-09280-2)
